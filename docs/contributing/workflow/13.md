## 13. Address all comments, questions, and suggestions.

<!-- eslint enable no-heading-punctuation -->
<!-- vale RedHat.HeadingPunctuation = YES -->
<!-- vale Google.HeadingPunctuation = YES -->
<!-- vale Microsoft.HeadingPunctuation = YES -->

When you submit a pull request you are likely to receive suggestions and feedback on your changes. When you do, the maintainer will convert your pull request back to a draft.

Responding to the suggestions and feedback that you receive is essential to having your changes merged into FarmData2. Whether the feedback comes from [a Copilot code review](./11.md) or a human FarmData2 maintainer the expectation is that you **carefully consider and respond to every comment and suggestion**.

- You might respond by:
  - agreeing with the suggestion, replying "I agree." and applying the suggestion as given.
  - disagreeing with the suggestion and explaining why you do not think it should be applied.
  - asking a follow up question about the suggestion.
  - giving an alternative change that you believe is better.
  - replying in any number of other ways that might lead to improvement of the pull request.

Note that the maintainer who reviews your pull request will change the title of your pull request to be a [conventional commit message](https://www.conventionalcommits.org/en/v1.0.0/). This title will have the form `type(scope): short title`. The _type_ and _scope_ specify the type and scope of changes that the pull request contains and are used for [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/).

After you have responded to the feedback:

1. [Commit the changes to your feature branch](./6.md).
2. [Push your feature branch to your fork on GitHub](./8.md).
3. [Mark your pull request as ready for review](./12.md).
4. You might also [mention (_tag_)](https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/writing-on-github/getting-started-with-writing-and-formatting-on-github/basic-writing-and-formatting-syntax#mentioning-people-and-teams) the maintainer who reviewed your pull request in a comment. This will notify them that you have reviewed and responded to the feedback, and that your pull request is ready for another review.

<!-- Allow "several" here -->

Taking a pull request from its initial form to being merged might require several rounds of feedback and revision. Responding to each round of feedback quickly will keep your work fresh in the maintainer's mind between reviews and improve the project's efficiency.

<!-- vale write-good.Weasel = YES -->

When the maintainer determines that your changes are ready to be merged, they will _[squash merge](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/incorporating-changes-from-a-pull-request/about-pull-request-merges#squash-and-merge-your-commits)_ your pull request. Squash merging compresses all commits in your pull request into a single commit. The pull request title and description become the commit title and message, and all attributions are added as commit trailers for the squash commit.
